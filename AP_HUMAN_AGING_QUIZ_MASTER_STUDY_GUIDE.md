**Question Stem:**
> Which of the following describes a concern for a country with an aging population?

**Answer Choices:**
* [ ] (A) As a country’s population ages, the life expectancy for the younger population of the country decreases and healthcare costs decline.
* [ ] (B) As a country’s population ages, the infant mortality rate decreases and there is an increased need for daycare centers.
* [x] **(C) As the proportion of older people in the country increases, fewer young workers are available to contribute tax revenues to support programs that provide services to the older population.** *(✅ Selected by Antony — CORRECT)*
* [ ] (D) As the proportion of older people in the country increases, the sex ratio becomes disproportionally skewed toward males.
* [ ] (E) As the proportion of older people increases, the total population decreases because older people do not have children.

> [!WARNING]
> **AP Trap Alert on Question 1 (The Aging Population Sex Ratio Trap)**:
> * **The Trap**: Distractor (D) claims that *"the sex ratio becomes disproportionately skewed toward males"*. Students who remember that "demographic imbalances alter sex ratios" often guess male without thinking about biological life expectancy.
> * **The Reality**: Worldwide, **women have longer average life expectancies than men by 4 to 7 years**. Consequently, as a population ages into cohorts $\ge 65$ and $\ge 80$, the sex ratio skews heavily toward **females** (often 2 to 3 elderly women for every elderly man), resulting in the "feminization of aging."
> * Distractor (C) correctly highlights the **shrinking tax base / dependency dilemma**: when the working cohort contracts relative to retirees, tax revenues decline precisely as medical/pension costs surge.

> [!WARNING]
> **AP Trap Alert on Question 2 (Pensions vs. Alternative Infrastructure Distractors)**:
> * **The Trap**: Distractor (B) claims declining youth leads to an *"increase in school construction"*, which is an inverse logical error. Distractor (D) claims politicians must appeal to *rural* voters, whereas Country A is explicitly specified as having a *"high percentage of urban population"*.

**Question Stem:**
> Which of the following would be most likely to increase life expectancy in a less developed country, resulting in an aging population in that country?

**Answer Choices:**
* [ ] (A) An increase in the country’s birth rate
* [ ] (B) Rural-to-urban migration within the country
* [ ] (C) Improvements in transportation and communication networks
* [x] **(D) Improvements in access to health care and sanitation** *(✅ Selected by Antony — CORRECT)*
* [ ] (E) A decrease in the country’s total fertility rate

> [!WARNING]
> **AP Trap Alert on Question 3 (Fertility Rate vs. Life Expectancy Cause & Effect)**:
> * **The Trap**: Distractor (E) mentions *"a decrease in the country's total fertility rate"*. While lower TFR changes the *proportion* of old people, it does not itself increase *individual life expectancy* (the average number of years a person is expected to live).
> * **The Reality**: Life expectancy rises strictly through reductions in mortality—specifically **clean piped water, sewage sanitation systems, childhood vaccines, and basic primary medical care** that drastically reduce infant and child death rates (the Epidemiological Transition from Stage 1/2 to Stage 3).
