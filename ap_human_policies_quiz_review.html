Consequence of one-child policy

VH926693 Skill 2.C ✓ 1 / 1 (Selected: C)

Which of the following explains an unintended consequence of China’s antinatalist one-child policy?

(A) A dramatic reduction of the birth rate and a decline of the rate of natural increase rate, resulting in lower population growth.
(B) Agricultural families were forced to relocate to the nearest city to search for employment, resulting in increased urban population.
✓ (C) A projected shortage of working-age people to care for the rising elderly population, resulting in an increase in the cost of living for older people. [Correct & Antony's Choice]
(D) Decreased spending on childhood expenses, such as education and nursery supplies results in a drop in domestic economic productivity.
(E) A decreased emphasis on education due to the decreasing numbers of school-age children results in a loss of international competitiveness.
💡 Pedagogical Deconstruction:

The dramatic limiting of births means younger generations are smaller than existing generations. As a result, there will be fewer workers to support pension and social security programs and provide care for the growing number of elderly citizens.

⚠️ AP Trap Alert: Intended Primary Goal vs. Unintended Consequence Trap

The Trap: Distractor (A) describes "a dramatic reduction of the birth rate and natural increase rate". This statement is 100% historically true, but it was the INTENDED GOAL of the Chinese government, not an unintended consequence!

The Reality & Rule of Thumb: When an AP question asks for unintended consequences of the One-Child Policy, look for:
• The 4-2-1 Problem: One child supporting two parents and four grandparents.
• Severe Sex Ratio Imbalance: Cultural son preference led to sex-selective abortion and an excess of ~30 million single males.
• Workforce Contraction: Shrinking manufacturing labor pool threatening economic growth.

Country likely to have pronatalist policies

VH926854 Skill 3.C ✓ 1 / 1 (Selected: B)

Which of the following country demographics would be used to explain the need for the pronatalist policies shown?

(A) A country with a birth rate of 43, a death rate of 12, and a rate of natural increase of 3.1%, passes a law that requires paid maternity leave.
✓ (B) A country with a birth rate of 11, a death rate of 13, and a rate of natural increase of −0.2%, legislates tax credits for working parents [Correct & Antony's Choice]
(C) A country with a birth rate of 16, a death rate of 4, and a rate of natural increase of 1.2%, establishes long maternity leave periods from work
(D) A country with a birth rate of 12, a death rate of 10, and a rate of natural increase of 0.2%, requires full-day kindergarten.
(E) A country with a birth rate of 20, a death rate of 10, and a rate of natural increase of 1.0%, provides state-subsidized day care centers.
💡 Pedagogical Deconstruction:

This country is the only one with a negative rate of natural increase (−0.2%). Countries with negative rates of natural increase and death rates that exceed birth rates have an acute need for pronatalist policies to incentivize larger families and stave off demographic collapse.

⚠️ AP Trap Alert: Pronatalist vs. Antinatalist Demographic Signal

The Reality & Rule of Thumb:
• Pronatalist Policies: Required by Stage 5 nations with negative NIR or near-zero growth (e.g., France, Japan, South Korea, Hungary). Look for death rates higher than birth rates ($CDR > CBR$).
• Antinatalist Policies: Required by Stage 2 nations with explosive NIR ($>2.5\%$) and high youth dependency (e.g., India historically, Kenya, Nigeria).

Reasons for antinatalist policies

VH926253 Skill 2.C ✓ 1 / 1 (Selected: E)

Country X has a high youth-dependency ratio. Country Z has fears of running out of agricultural resources. Which of the following explains why Countries X and Z would implement anti-natalist policies?

(A) Antinatalist policies promote higher birth rates so the country will have an adequate future workforce.
(B) Antinatalist policies promote immigration so the country will be able to sustain its population.
(C) Antinatalist policies correct skewed sex ratios so the number of each sex will be relatively even.
(D) Antinatalist policies promote health and sanitation to lower the number of deaths from infectious diseases.
✓ (E) Antinatalist policies promote family planning strategies to reduce fertility rates and discourage population growth. [Correct & Antony's Choice]
💡 Pedagogical Deconstruction:

Both countries want to reduce fertility rates and overall population growth to relieve pressure on public services (schools, clinics) and agricultural carrying capacity.

⚠️ AP Trap Alert: Antinatalist Mechanism vs. Inverted Outcome

The Trap: Distractor (C) claims antinatalist policies "correct skewed sex ratios". In reality, strict government antinatalism in patriarchal cultures (e.g., China's One-Child Policy) worsened skewed sex ratios due to illegal sex determination and ultrasound female feticide.
