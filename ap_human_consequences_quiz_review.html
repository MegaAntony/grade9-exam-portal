Effect of migration patterns in Brazil

VH929906 Skill 2.C ✓ Selected: (C)
Stimulus Analysis: Proportional Symbol Map

• Dense Atlantic Coastal Strip: Massive urban symbols for São Paulo (10M+), Rio de Janeiro, Salvador, Recife, and Fortaleza.

• Interior Core: Planned federal capital Brasília situated inland to encourage westward migration.

• Amazonian Periphery: Manaus isolated along the Amazon River with sparse surrounding density.

Which of the following best explains a likely effect of Brazil’s population distribution?

(A) More uniform economic growth throughout the entire country as urban areas expand westward
(B) Increased cultural unity as more people live in close proximity to one another in eastern Brazil
✓ (C) Uneven economic development as eastern Brazil experiences more growth than the sparsely inhabited western regions [Correct & Antony's Choice]
(D) The expansion of available farmland as people move to the cities, allowing more rural land to be farmed
(E) More efficient political administration and improved governance as the population becomes more clustered
💡 AP Human Geography Geographic Rationale:

Because historical colonization and modern industrial investment clustered along the Atlantic coast, the southeast represents Brazil’s economic core. The vast western interior lacks equivalent infrastructure, transportation links, and industrial capital, creating persistent regional disparities in GDP per capita and standard of living.

⚠️ AP Trap Alert: Spatial Disparities vs. Uniform Growth Trap

The Trap: Distractor (A) claims "more uniform economic growth throughout the entire country as urban areas expand westward". Many students fall for the trap of thinking that building a forward capital (like Brasília) or urbanization automatically balances development across a country.

The Reality & Rule of Thumb: Brazil's population remains heavily clustered along the Atlantic coastal strip. This clustering generates persistent core-periphery inequality (the southeast represents Brazil’s economic core, while the western interior and Amazonian periphery face acute infrastructure gaps). Distractor (B) also traps students by claiming "increased cultural unity"—clustering in reality intensifies socioeconomic segregation (favelas vs. wealthy high-rises).

Physiological density and carrying capacity

VH932892 Skill 2.C ✓ Selected: (B)

Which of the following explains why a country with a high physiological density (e.g., Japan, Egypt, or the Netherlands) risks exceeding its carrying capacity?

(A) The population is clustered in key areas, so rural populations will not have easy access to public services such as schools and hospitals.
✓ (B) The population places a large amount of pressure on the arable land to produce enough food without risking severe environmental degradation. [Correct & Antony's Choice]
(C) The population is evenly dispersed throughout the country, so it is difficult to maintain infrastructure such as roads and telecommunication networks for the entire country.
(D) There is a large number of people per unit of total land area, so there is no available land for farmers to raise crops and other food products.
(E) The amount of farmers per unit of land area in these countries is too low to be able to support their populations.
💡 AP Human Geography Geographic Rationale:

Physiological density measures population relative to arable land (farmable soil). A high physiological density indicates severe pressure on agricultural ecosystems. Intensive farming leads to soil exhaustion, erosion, and salinization, jeopardizing long-term carrying capacity unless supported by high-tech inputs or food imports.

⚠️ AP Trap Alert: Density Confusion Trap (Arithmetic vs. Physiological vs. Agricultural)

The Trap: Distractors (D) and (E) prey on students who memorize the word "density" without checking the exact denominator:
• Distractor (D) defines Arithmetic Density ($\frac{\text{Total People}}{\text{Total Land}}$).
• Distractor (E) defines Agricultural Density ($\frac{\text{Farmers}}{\text{Arable Land}}$) and falsely claims developed nations like Japan or Netherlands have "too few farmers to support their populations" (ignoring mechanization and global trade).

The Reality & Rule of Thumb: Physiological Density is specifically $\frac{\text{Total Population}}{\text{Arable Land}}$. A high ratio means intense pressure on farmable soil. When arable land is pushed past ecological limits to feed a dense population, the country risks exceeding its carrying capacity through soil salinization, chemical contamination, and erosion.

Urban Population in MDCs

VH911513 Skill 2.C ✓ Selected: (A)

The increasing percentage of urban dwellers in more-developed countries is best explained by

✓ (A) greater access to job opportunities, resources, and facilities [Correct & Antony's Choice]
(B) a critical need for workers in the primary economic sector
(C) an abundance of arable land for agricultural use
(D) a desire for more living space and a slower lifestyle
(E) inadequate health care and few educational resources
💡 AP Human Geography Geographic Rationale:

In post-industrial economies (MDCs), tertiary (service) and quaternary (knowledge-based) sectors flourish in urban agglomerations. Cities provide high-paying corporate, research, financial, and healthcare employment alongside advanced infrastructure, drawing rural and suburban dwellers toward metropolitan hubs.

⚠️ AP Trap Alert: Economic Sector & Counterurbanization Trap

The Trap:
• Distractor (B) claims cities grow due to a "need for workers in the primary economic sector". The primary sector (farming, mining) is rural, not urban, and is heavily mechanized in MDCs.
• Distractor (D) claims people move to cities for "more living space and a slower lifestyle". That describes counterurbanization / suburban exurban flight, which is the exact opposite of urban agglomeration.

The Reality & Rule of Thumb: Urban growth in MDCs is propelled by the expansion of tertiary (service) and quaternary (information/finance) agglomerations. Cities offer specialized, high-wage jobs, universities, and advanced healthcare networks that act as powerful economic pull factors.
