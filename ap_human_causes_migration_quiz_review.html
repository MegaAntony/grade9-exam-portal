Emigration to start a business

VH926935 Skill 2.C ✓ 1 / 1 (Selected: B)

Which of the following types of migration are correctly explained by the example given?

(A) International migration and forced migration: Family A from Beijing moves to Minneapolis so their children can attend an American university.
✓ (B) International migration and voluntary migration: Family A from Mumbai has convinced their close friends Family B to emigrate to London as they did in order help start up their business. [Correct & Antony's Choice]
(C) Internal migration and forced migration: Couple A from Berlin has convinced their close friends Couple B to move to Greece and live in the same retirement community.
(D) Internal migration and voluntary migration: Family A from Baghdad are resettled by the United States for political asylum in Detroit.
(E) Internally displaced persons and forced migration: Couple A from rural Brazil move to São Paulo to find better job opportunities.
💡 Pedagogical Deconstruction:

This is a correct example of international migration (crossing from India to the United Kingdom) and voluntary migration (freely choosing to relocate to start an enterprise and pursue economic opportunity).

⚠️ AP Trap Alert: The Two-Variable Matrix Trap

The Trap: Distractors swap one variable while keeping the other accurate:
• Distractor (A): Moving for university is voluntary, not forced.
• Distractor (C): Berlin to Greece is international, not internal.
• Distractor (D): Baghdad to Detroit is international, not internal.
• Distractor (E): Moving for jobs is economic voluntary, not IDP forced migration.

Pull factor for asylum seekers

VH929891 Skill 2.C ✓ 1 / 1 (Selected: E)

In 2018, a large number of migrants from Afghanistan and Iraq applied for asylum in the European Union. Which of the following is a pull factor that explains this migration?

(A) A high rate of natural increase in the Middle East, leading to overpopulation
(B) Armed conflict, driving many people from their home regions in the Middle East
(C) Drought and food shortages in the Middle East, leading many people to search for food security
(D) Immigration reforms and fenced borders around Europe, controlling the flow of migrant workers
✓ (E) Economic growth and employment opportunities in Europe, attracting immigrant workers [Correct & Antony's Choice]
💡 Pedagogical Deconstruction:

Economic growth and employment opportunities are pull factors that attract people to relocate to European destination countries. Armed conflict and drought in the Middle East are powerful push factors, not pull factors.

⚠️ AP Trap Alert: Push Factor vs. Pull Factor Wording Trap

The Trap: Distractor (B) states "Armed conflict, driving many people from their home regions in the Middle East". Students immediately recognize armed conflict as the main reason people leave Iraq and Afghanistan, and bubble (B) instantly.

The Reality: Armed conflict is a PUSH FACTOR (forcing people away from home). The question specifically asks for a PULL FACTOR (attracting people into Europe). Only Option (E) describes an attraction factor!

Push - pull factors for refugees

VH930357 Skill 2.C ✓ 1 / 1 (Selected: E)

The international Syrian refugee crisis that started in 2011 is best explained by which of the following migration factors?

(A) An economic pull factor from the European Union, where jobs and housing are available in cities
(B) A demographic push factor from Syria, where the total fertility rate is high
(C) A political pull factor from the United States, where there is a history of Syrian immigration
(D) A political pull factor from Turkey, where there are free and open elections
✓ (E) A political push factor from Syria, where armed conflict imperils personal safety [Correct & Antony's Choice]
💡 Pedagogical Deconstruction:

Refugees, by definition under the 1951 Geneva Convention, flee because of well-founded fear of persecution, conflict, violence, or human rights violations. The Syrian civil war created severe political push factors that threatened personal survival.

⚠️ AP Trap Alert: Economic Motive vs. Refugee Legal Definition

The Trap: Distractor (A) describes economic pull factors. While refugees certainly need jobs upon arrival, refugees are legally distinguished from economic migrants because their movement is forced by political violence and threat to life.
